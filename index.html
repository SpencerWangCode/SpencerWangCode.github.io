About

My research is centred on computational psychiatry, with a particular interest in synthetic-agent modelling and the conditions under which behavioural simulations can support mechanistic explanation. Philosophically, my work is grounded in post-positivist analytic philosophy of science, especially Carl F. Craver’s mechanistic philosophy of science and James F. Woodward’s interventionist account of causation.

Alongside academic research, I have a sustained interest in religious thought, particularly Madhyamaka Buddhism and Christian process theology, as resources for thinking about selfhood, suffering, agency, process, and transformation.
